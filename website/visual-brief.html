From fish-eDNA data to a defensible claim

We began with five plausible interpretations, tried to falsify each one, and retained only the conclusions their evidence could carry.

01 · Our question

Which conclusions can the evidence actually support?

What remains defensible after correcting the unit of inference, testing imperfect-detection recovery, and auditing pipeline, taxonomic and trait uncertainty?

The source study supplied the samples and original data. This project added the reproducible reanalysis and claim-audit layer.

03 · Initial claims

Five hypotheses from the first analytical phase

Downstream detected-label broadening is robust.

A correction model recovers rare-label Conwy occupancy.

Lower source signal and processed fish counts predict pipeline non-recovery.

Longer markers resolve Salmo species better than MiFish.

Trait diversity broadens beyond richness and prevalence alone.

These were claims formulated by this reanalysis, not claims attributed to the source-study authors.

04 · Stressing the claims

Each claim received one of three decisions

ACCEPTED = met the declared decision rule · REJECTED = an adequate test did not support the claim · NON-CONCLUSIVE = the current data or model could not decide it.

06 · Final verdict

The evidence narrowed—not erased—the result

ACCEPTED: the bounded comparator-relative pipeline association. NON-CONCLUSIVE: robust downstream broadening, rare-label Conwy occupancy correction and longer-marker Salmo species resolution. REJECTED: trait broadening beyond richness and prevalence.

Scientific addition: a reproducible credibility-audit framework whose questions, tests, and verdict rules were written in a dated project plan before analysis, giving each initial claim one transparent decision and its reason.
